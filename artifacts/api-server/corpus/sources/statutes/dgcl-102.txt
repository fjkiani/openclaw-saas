DELAWARE GENERAL CORPORATION LAW §102 — CONTENTS OF CERTIFICATE OF INCORPORATION

Source: Delaware Code Online, Title 8, Chapter 1, Subchapter II. 8 Del. C. §102.

(a) The certificate of incorporation shall contain the following: (1) The name of the corporation, which shall contain 1 of the words "association," "company," "corporation," "club," "foundation," "fund," "incorporated," "institute," "society," "union," "syndicate," or "limited," (or abbreviations thereof, with or without punctuation), or words (or abbreviations thereof, with or without punctuation) of like import of countries other than this State (provided that they are written in roman characters or letters); (2) The address, including street, city and zip code, of the corporation's registered office in this State, and the name of its registered agent at such address; (3) The nature of the business or purposes to be conducted or promoted by the corporation. It shall be sufficient to state, either alone or with other businesses or purposes, that the purpose of the corporation is to engage in any lawful act or activity for which corporations may be organized under the General Corporation Law of Delaware, and by such statement all lawful acts and activities shall be within the purposes of the corporation, except for those unlawful acts and activities which are specifically excluded; (4) The total number of shares of stock which the corporation shall have authority to issue and, if the shares are to be divided into classes, a description of the classes of shares and a statement of the total number of shares of each class which the corporation shall have authority to issue.

(b) In addition to the matters required to be set forth in the certificate of incorporation by subsection (a) of this section, the certificate of incorporation may also contain any or all of the following matters: (1) Any provision for the management of the business and for the conduct or regulation of the affairs of the corporation, including any provision that creates, varies, defines or limits the duties, responsibilities or liability of directors; (2) Any provision creating, defining, limiting or regulating the powers of the corporation, the directors and the stockholders, or any class of the stockholders, or the members of a nonstock corporation; provided that no such provision shall be inconsistent with law; (3) Any provision which, under this chapter, may be set forth in bylaws; (4) Any provision requiring, for any corporate action, the vote of a larger portion of the stock or of any class or series thereof, or of a larger number of the directors, or the vote of any additional parties, than is required by this chapter; (5) Any provision limiting the duration of the corporation's existence.

(c) It shall not be necessary to set forth in the certificate of incorporation any of the powers enumerated in this chapter.

(d) In addition to the matters set forth in subsections (a) and (b) of this section, the certificate of incorporation may also contain: (1) A provision eliminating or limiting the personal liability of a director to the corporation or its stockholders for monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty as a director, provided that such provision shall not eliminate or limit the liability of a director (i) for any breach of the director's duty of loyalty to the corporation or its stockholders, (ii) for acts or omissions not in good faith or which involve intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law, (iii) under §174 of this title, or (iv) for any transaction from which the director derived an improper personal benefit.

ANALYSIS INSTRUCTION: When reviewing a Delaware corporation's certificate of incorporation, verify that all §102(a) required elements are present. Check for §102(b)(1) director liability limitations and §102(d)(1) exculpation provisions. Flag if the certificate omits a §102(d)(1) exculpation clause — this is standard for venture-backed startups and its absence increases director personal liability exposure.
