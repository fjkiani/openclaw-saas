DELAWARE GENERAL CORPORATION LAW §144 — INTERESTED DIRECTORS AND OFFICERS; CONTROLLING STOCKHOLDER TRANSACTIONS; QUORUM

Source: Delaware Code Online, Title 8, Chapter 1, Subchapter IV. 8 Del. C. §144.

(a) Except for a controlling stockholder transaction under subsection (b) or (c) of this section, an act or transaction involving or between a corporation, or one or more of the corporation's subsidiaries, on the one hand, and one or more of the corporation's directors or officers, on the other hand, or involving or between a corporation or one or more of the corporation's subsidiaries, on the one hand, and any other corporation, partnership, limited liability company, statutory trust, association, or any other entity or organization in which one or more of its directors or officers are directors, stockholders, partners, managers, members, or officers, or have a financial interest, on the other hand, may not be the subject of equitable relief, or give rise to an award of damages, against a director or officer of the corporation because of the foregoing circumstances or the receipt of any benefit by any such director, officer, entity, or organization or because the director or officer is present at or participates in the meeting of the board or committee which authorizes the act or transaction or was involved in the initiation, negotiation, or approval of the act or transaction (including by virtue of a director's vote being counted for such purpose), if:

(1) The material facts as to the director's or officer's relationship or interest and as to the act or transaction, including any involvement in the initiation, negotiation, or approval of the act or transaction, are disclosed or are known to all members of the board of directors or a committee of the board of directors, and the board or committee in good faith and without gross negligence authorizes the act or transaction by the affirmative votes of a majority of the disinterested directors then serving on the board of directors or such committee, even though the disinterested directors be less than a quorum; provided that if a majority of the directors are not disinterested directors with respect to the act or transaction, such act or transaction shall be approved (or recommended for approval) by a committee of the board of directors that consists of two or more directors, each of whom the board of directors has determined to be a disinterested director with respect to the act or transaction; or

(2) The act or transaction is approved or ratified by an informed, uncoerced, affirmative vote of a majority of the votes cast by the stockholders entitled to vote thereon, other than votes controlled by any person described in paragraph (a)(1) of this section; or

(3) The act or transaction, judged according to the circumstances at the time of commitment, is established to have been fair to the corporation as of the time it was authorized, approved or ratified, as the case may be, by the board of directors, a committee or the stockholders.

(b) A transaction between a controlling stockholder and the corporation (including, without limitation, the acquisition by a controlling stockholder of any asset or any class or series of stock of the corporation, a merger, consolidation or share exchange with the corporation, or any other transaction that results in the controlling stockholder receiving a financial benefit not received by the other stockholders) may not be the subject of equitable relief, or give rise to an award of damages, against the controlling stockholder because of the controlling stockholder's circumstances or position, if:

(1) The transaction is approved by a committee of the board of directors that consists of two or more directors, each of whom the board of directors has determined to be a disinterested director with respect to the transaction, and the committee in good faith and without gross negligence determines that the transaction is fair to the corporation as of the time it is authorized, approved or ratified; or

(2) The transaction is approved by an informed, uncoerced, affirmative vote of a majority of the votes cast by the stockholders entitled to vote thereon, other than votes controlled by the controlling stockholder; or

(3) The transaction, judged according to the circumstances at the time of commitment, is established to have been entirely fair to the corporation as of the time it was authorized, approved or ratified, as the case may be, by the board of directors, a committee or the stockholders.

(c) For purposes of this section, a "controlling stockholder" means a stockholder who, at the time of the transaction, possesses, directly or indirectly, either (i) the power to exercise control over the corporation through ownership of a majority of the voting power of the corporation's stock, or (ii) the power to exercise control over the corporation through any other means, including, without limitation, the power to elect a majority of the directors of the corporation.

(d) Common or interested directors may be counted in determining the presence of a quorum at a meeting of the board of directors or of a committee which authorizes the act or transaction.

ANALYSIS INSTRUCTION: When analyzing Delaware-incorporated founder agreements, RSAs, RSPAs, or stock restriction agreements, evaluate compliance with DGCL §144 safe harbor conditions for interested-director transactions. If the agreement involves a controlling stockholder, apply the heightened standards of subsections (b) and (c). Flag any transaction where interested directors approve without disinterested director or stockholder ratification.
